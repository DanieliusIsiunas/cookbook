# Creamy guanciale tagliatelle

## Intended result

Glossy tagliatelle coated in a rich egg-and-cheese sauce, with golden guanciale and freshly ground black pepper. Simple, restaurant-style flavour without cream. Makes three regular or two generous portions.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Preparation or specification |
|---|---:|---|
| Tagliatelle | 300 g | Dried pasta is the base assumption; fresh pasta was not separately tested |
| Guanciale | 100 g | Cut into roughly 5 mm strips |
| Whole egg | 1 | Pasteurised |
| Additional egg yolks | 3 | Pasteurised |
| Hard cheese | 70 g | Very finely grated; cheese variety not recorded |
| Freshly ground black pepper | 0.5 teaspoon | Increase up to 1 teaspoon to taste |
| Salt | 5 g per litre of pasta water | Starting concentration; adjust for salty cheese and guanciale |
| Reserved pasta water | About 150 ml | Reserve a mug; add only as needed |
| Extra hard cheese | A little | For serving |

## Equipment

- Wide frying pan, pasta pot, large mixing bowl and tongs.
- Fine grater and a mug for reserving pasta water.

## Method

1. Put the guanciale in a cold, wide frying pan. Cook over medium-low heat for 8-12 minutes, until the fat renders and the edges turn golden with a tender centre. Remove the pieces, leaving about two tablespoons of rendered fat in the pan.
2. In a large mixing bowl, whisk the whole egg, three yolks, 70 g finely grated cheese and most of the pepper into a cohesive sauce base.
3. Cook the tagliatelle in lightly salted water, starting at 5 g salt per litre, until just firm to the bite. Reserve a mug of cooking water before draining.
4. Add the drained pasta and two tablespoons of pasta water to the guanciale pan. Toss over low heat for about 30 seconds to coat the ribbons, then remove from the heat.
5. Transfer the hot pasta into the bowl of egg-and-cheese mixture and immediately toss vigorously. Add reserved pasta water one tablespoon at a time until the sauce becomes glossy and flows between the ribbons. Fold in most of the guanciale.
6. Serve immediately on warm plates. Finish with the remaining guanciale, extra cheese and pepper. Leave the sauce slightly looser than the desired final texture because it thickens during serving.

## Sensory checkpoints

- Guanciale has golden edges, rendered fat and a tender centre, without burnt bitterness.
- Pasta remains just firm to the bite before the pan toss.
- Finished sauce coats the ribbons smoothly, with no egg curds or greasy pool.
- Sauce flows between the ribbons rather than sticking in a thick paste.

## Chef tips

- The pan toss coats the pasta with guanciale flavour; the separate bowl lets residual pasta heat thicken the egg sauce with less risk of scrambling it.
- Finely grated cheese melts more readily. Starchy pasta water helps bring the fat, cheese and eggs into a glossy sauce.
- Add water gradually: the reserved amount is a supply, not a target to pour in.

## Critical variables

- Keep the egg mixture away from direct pan heat.
- Transfer and toss promptly while the pasta is hot.
- Rendered fat, cheese saltiness and the amount of water needed vary by product.
- Timings and yield are estimates from the supplied recipe, not measured during the successful cook.

## Proven variations

No substitutions or variations have been tested separately. This recipe does not establish the earlier historical fettuccine or smoked-bacon adaptation.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Sauce is thick or sticky | Too little pasta water or delayed serving | Toss in warm pasta water one tablespoon at a time and serve promptly |
| Egg curds appear | Excessive heat | Use the separate bowl and avoid direct heat; scrambled sauce cannot be fully reversed |
| Sauce is greasy | Excess rendered fat or insufficient emulsification | Leave about two tablespoons of fat in the pan; toss vigorously with small additions of starchy water |
| Dish is too salty | Salty guanciale and cheese plus heavily salted water | Lightly salt the cooking water and taste before adding any final salt |

## Serving and storage

Best eaten immediately. Use pasteurised eggs because residual pasta heat does not reliably fully cook them. Refrigerate leftovers within two hours; reheating will change the sauce texture.

Allergens: wheat, eggs and milk.
