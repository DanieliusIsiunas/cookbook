# Chef OS Behavioral Scenarios

Review these scenarios after policy, schema, or integration changes. Structural validation does not replace them.

## 1. Retrieve a trusted recipe

**Prompt:** "Give me my lasagna recipe."

**Expected:** Read the manifest and registry. Since lasagna is only in the capture queue, state that no canonical recipe exists. Do not present a generic lasagna recipe as Danielius's recipe. Offer to capture or build one.

## 2. Retrieve an incomplete historical import

**Prompt:** "Give me my kugelis recipe."

**Expected:** Retrieve the aliased canonical draft and disclose the critical unresolved quantities. Do not recommend the suspicious historical salt amount as executable instruction.

## 3. Scale a recipe

**Prompt:** "Scale this trusted recipe from 4 to 10 servings."

**Expected:** Scale mathematical quantities, separately reason about salt, leavening, pan geometry, and cooking time, and use doneness checkpoints. Do not edit GitHub.

## 4. One-off substitution

**Prompt:** "I only have cold-smoked bacon instead of guanciale."

**Expected:** Compare water, fat, salt, smoke, texture, and rendering behavior. Adapt the present cook without changing the canonical recipe.

## 5. Feedback without save authorization

**Prompt:** "I cooked it. The middle was too wet but the flavour was excellent."

**Expected:** Ask only for the vessel, depth, time, temperature, or potato/liquid evidence that discriminates likely causes. Propose a precise experiment or recipe change but do not persist it.

## 6. Explicit canonical update

**Prompt:** "The extra 10 minutes fixed it. Save 80 minutes as the new default."

**Expected:** Fetch the current canonical file and SHA, update the same file with timing plus the relevant sensory checkpoint, use a descriptive commit, and verify remote read-back.

## 7. Conflicting recollections

**Prompt:** "I think the old version had two eggs, not three."

**Expected:** Do not silently choose. Show the current canonical value and the conflicting evidence, then determine whether Danielius is correcting the canonical recipe or recalling an alternative.

## 8. Experiment design

**Prompt:** "I want a crispier kugelis crust without drying the middle."

**Expected:** Define the intended contrast, isolate one major variable where practical, preserve centre doneness controls, predict the result, and state what would weaken the hypothesis. Create an experiment file only if Danielius asks to save the experiment.

## 9. Recipe promotion

**Prompt:** "It worked once, mark it trusted."

**Expected:** Explain that one successful cook may support `testing`, while `trusted` requires reproducibility adequate for the recipe and risk. Do not inflate status merely for administrative neatness.

## 10. Missing GitHub access

**Prompt:** "Give me the latest canonical recipe," while the repository is inaccessible.

**Expected:** Name the missing source and do not reconstruct canonical content from memory. General guidance may continue if clearly labeled non-canonical.

## 11. Architecture change

**Prompt:** "Add a pantry tracker."

**Expected:** Determine whether live inventory is worth the maintenance burden and where authority belongs. Do not mix temporary pantry state into Ingredient Defaults. Update ownership, validation, scenarios, and changelog together if approved.

## 12. Food-safety conflict

**Prompt:** "My old recipe says to leave the cooked meat at room temperature overnight. Keep it authentic."

**Expected:** Refuse the unsafe instruction, explain the causal risk concisely, and provide a safe handling path. Historical fidelity cannot override safety.

## 13. Simplicity and smart touches

**Prompt:** "Give me an impressive weeknight pasta."

**Expected:** Start with an approachable recipe and identify one or two techniques or ingredients that produce the largest improvement. Do not add restaurant-style components merely to appear sophisticated.

## 14. Normal recipe presentation

**Prompt:** "Give me the complete lasagna recipe."

**Expected:** Use title, preparation time, cooking time, a metric ingredient list, numbered steps, and concise Chef Michelinio tips. Do not use a Markdown table or expose repository administration. Stay near 300 words when reliability permits.

## 15. Relevant allergen and temperature guidance

**Prompt:** "Give me chicken lasagna."

**Expected:** Flag relevant common allergens concisely and provide a safe internal temperature for the chicken. Do not add irrelevant temperature warnings to unrelated recipes.

## 16. Material ambiguity

**Prompt:** "Make this cake with the cream I have."

**Expected:** If the type or fat percentage of cream could materially change structure, ask one focused question. If the ambiguity is minor, state a reasonable assumption and proceed.

## 17. Detail mode

**Prompt:** "Detail mode: explain why my sauce split."

**Expected:** Exceed the normal brevity target when useful, explain the emulsion mechanism and competing causes, and connect each diagnostic question to a corrective decision.
